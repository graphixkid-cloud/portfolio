01 · Verizon

Verizon Family

Consolidating five legacy family products into one app, and turning NPS around by 20 points in a year.

My role
Led a 30-person cross-functional design organization across five autonomous product squads: 8 full-time designers, 8 contractors and 14 agency partners from Publicis Sapient.
Scale
950K+ paying subscriptions; about 1.2M users migrating from five legacy products
Timeline
3-month redesign sprint; unified app launched H2 2024

The problem

Verizon's family products were spread across five separate apps: Smart Family, GizmoHub, Care Smart, Hum and Roadside. Each carried years of accumulated settings, nothing gave the household a shared picture, and third-party vendor latency slowed everything down. Sentiment showed it: Smart Family NPS hovered between −22 and −26, with Android as low as −31.

Key decisions

  1. Cut legacy features instead of porting them. Product managers and squad leads pushed hard for 1:1 feature parity to keep power users happy. I held the line on strict deprecation and backed it with rapid qualitative testing, which showed leadership that legacy setting clutter was the main source of user confusion and negative reviews. We sunset the low-value controls and simplified the core information architecture.
  2. Organize around family profiles, not devices. Location, place alerts, safety check-ins, group communication and driving insights all hang off a person, so the app still makes sense when a child changes phones or gets a watch.
  3. Set feature depth by age and role. Each capability surfaces at the depth that fits the member: tight controls for a young child, lighter check-ins and driving insights for a teen.

Outcome

+20 ptsNPS swing in 12 months, to −1 to +2
4.4–4.8★App store ratings
−2.47%Net churn

Measured launch to launch over 12 months, overall NPS rose about 20 points to between −1 and +2, and iOS NPS climbed from −18 to as high as +10. Simpler onboarding and a cleaner daily experience drove the recovery, and new activations outpaced cancellations, so net churn turned negative across 950K+ subscriptions.

02 · EBS BrokerTec (now CME Group)

One screen for spot and forwards

An HTML5 trading screen that brought EBS Market spot and the new EBS Direct FX Forwards into one place, validated on the trading floor.

My role
Senior User Experience Designer, 2012–2017. Embedded with trading product and platform engineering; designed the new HTML5 trading front end.
Scale
$41B average daily volume in EBS Direct FX Forwards, alongside EBS Market spot at $100B+ a day

The problem

EBS was expanding beyond its central limit order book for spot into bilateral liquidity with EBS Direct, a new platform for FX Forwards (outrights and currency swaps). Forwards still ran largely on phone and chat, and traders were already split across a native Windows C++ app, an Adobe Flex app and early HTML: three interaction models for one high-stakes task repeated thousands of times a day.

Key decisions

  1. Put both markets on one screen. Blue price cards carry EBS Market spot; green cards carry EBS Direct bilateral liquidity. Traders read both sources side by side without switching applications.
  2. Lay out the screen by the trader's scan path. High-density pricing grids, depth ladders and order entry are placed for peripheral reading, so a trader can watch several pairs while acting on one.
  3. Redesign iceberg orders for speed. Traders slice, hide and execute large-block spot orders through high-density tiles and hotkeys instead of a multi-step modal. This workflow drove a 30% acceleration in Spot FX on $100B average trading volume.
  4. Turn multi-leg tickets into one action. Forward and swap quotes that took a phone call or chat thread and a multi-field ticket became single-click responses and custom hotkeys.
  5. Build the safety rails into the ticket. Automated pre-trade credit checks, pre-populated deal templates and strict tolerance bounds replaced manual entry, removing the source of fat-finger errors on multi-million-dollar notionals.

How we validated it

On-floor usability sessions with active institutional bank traders, FX desk heads and liquidity providers in London and New York. We tested the pricing grids, depth ladders and hotkey execution flows against split-second trading under heavy volatility.

Outcome

$41BADV, EBS Direct FX Forwards
10,000Active institutional users
30%Spot FX acceleration from the iceberg redesign

The platform quickly reached 10,000 active institutional users across tier-1 market-making banks and liquidity providers. Single-click quotes and hotkeys replaced phone and chat negotiation, automated credit checks removed manual ticket errors, and EBS won "Best Broker for Spot FX" five years in a row.

03 · Verizon

Verizon Gizmo Watch

A smartwatch worn by a six-year-old and bought, set up and trusted by a parent.

My role
Lead / Principal Experience Designer for GizmoWatch 2 and GizmoWatch 3: end-to-end UX/UI architecture, hardware-software interaction, on-watch OS navigation and the GizmoHub companion app. Also authored the UX analysis and product roadmap.
Scale
700K+ active parent accounts managing 2M+ connected family contacts

The problem

The watch is worn by a child but bought, configured and trusted by a parent. Every new feature had to be simple enough for a six-year-old's wrist and clear enough to earn a parent's confidence from the companion app.

Key decisions

  1. Design every feature as a pair. Each feature (Ask & Learn AI assistant, weather, alerts, video calling and texting, water lock, to-do lists) has an on-watch interaction and a GizmoHub counterpart for the parent, designed together.
  2. Keep the wrist simple and put control with the parent. The watch shows a child only what they need in the moment. Setup, rules and trusted contacts (up to 10–20 per watch, including multiple guardians) live in GizmoHub.
  3. Design for a child who grows. The feature set extends the watch's useful life as children age into it, instead of being outgrown in a year.

Outcome

Forbes Vetted named the Gizmo Watch 4 its Best Kids Smartwatch Overall in 2026 after hands-on family testing. 04 · Verizon

Verizon Care Smart

Independence for an older adult and reassurance for their family, without making either feel monitored.

My role
Principal Experience Designer for the watch experience and the family companion app.
Audience
Older adults living independently (the wearer), and the adult children and family who act as caregivers
Timeline
Launched March 2020; expanded to Apple Watch with 24/7 professional monitoring and fall detection (2021–22) and Samsung Galaxy Watch4+ on Wear OS (Q2 2023)

The problem

An older adult wants independence, not supervision. Their adult child wants reassurance without intruding. One product had to serve both people without making either feel watched, and an emergency feature had to be fast without being easy to trigger by mistake.

Key decisions

  1. Keep the watch radically simple. High-contrast text, simplified circular touch targets, a small fixed set of trusted contacts and plain language.
  2. Make Get Help intentional. Testing with older adults showed frequent false emergency dispatches from accidental taps on the prominent emergency button. We replaced the single tap with a slider, which eliminated accidental dispatches without slowing access in a real emergency.
  3. Move the complexity to the family app. Configuration, medication reminders, check-in status and remote Medical ID management live with the caregiver, along with low-friction location alerts and fall notifications.
  4. Frame safety as a companion, not a monitor. Fall detection, location and the Trust Circle are presented as support the wearer chooses, with group chat keeping the family connected day to day.

How we validated it

We tested with both sides of the dual-user model: senior wearers, focusing on contrast, touch targets and accidental-trigger prevention, and family caregivers, who needed fast location alerts, fall notifications and remote Medical ID management.

Outcome

0Accidental emergency dispatches after the slider redesign
3Watch platforms: Care Smart, Apple Watch, Galaxy Watch
24/7Professional monitoring with fall detection

Care Smart grew from a dedicated senior smartwatch into a service across Apple and Samsung wearables, extending Verizon's wearable portfolio to a new life stage.

05 · Self-directed, 2026

FinFront UI SDK

One designer, five financial verticals, 75 components, and no room for drift between the docs and the product. Built Claude-first.

My role
Sole designer and product lead: research, IA, visual language, tokens, component behavior and documentation
Scope
75 components · 5 verticals (FX/Prop, RIA, CRE & Alts, Commodities, Core) · 17 user roles · 3 device targets
Note
A self-directed prototype for portfolio demonstration, not a licensed product. All data is synthetic.

The problem

A trading terminal, an advisor workspace and a GP capital-raise desk all need to agree on color, numbers and what a severity flag means, without becoming the same app in five costumes. Meanwhile, trading platforms keep rebuilding the same order books and blotters, and private markets (cap rates, target IRR, GP/LP waterfalls) have almost no designed patterns at all.

Key decisions

  1. Make the library the source of truth, not a record of it. Every token, class and component is extracted from the five working prototypes' production CSS, so documentation can't drift from what it documents. Tokens follow a three-tier model (primitive, semantic, component), generated from JSON.
  2. Share foundations, scope the components. All verticals inherit one token layer, but each scopes its own component classes under a platform wrapper, so RIA's severity chip never collides with CRE's. A sixth vertical follows the rule instead of inventing a new one.
  3. Keep two color systems apart on purpose. One severity ladder (critical to low) runs across Prop Desk, RIA and Commodities. CRE & Alts gets its own capital-lifecycle colors (uncalled, invested, distributed), because forcing it onto the severity ladder would make two unrelated ideas compete for the same color. I logged that as a decision rather than quietly overloading a token.
  4. Document live, not static. Every component in the library is interactive. Switching theme, density or colorblind-safe mode restyles every demo across all five verticals at once, because the library runs on the same CSS custom properties as the prototypes.

How I worked with AI

I directed the research synthesis, personas and IA, visual language and tokens, and component behavior and interaction rules. Claude drafted fintech requirements docs, React / TypeScript and CSS scaffolding, and generated and QA'd the library sections, which let five verticals iterate in days instead of months. Every deliverable went through a headless render-and-check pass before it shipped.

Where I overrode it. The first render of the FX trading screen looked plausible but didn't hold up to a trader's eye: visual elements were misaligned, the column-customization controls in the upper right didn't work, and the RFQ buttons did nothing. The root problem was that Claude treated RFQ as one generic flow. I walked it through how the request-for-quote workflow differs by asset type, had it rebuild against those workflows, and re-checked every control before moving on. AI got me to a first draft fast; domain knowledge decided what was actually correct.

Outcome

75Live, documented components
5Working sector prototypes
17User roles designed for

A headless React 19 / TypeScript library and design system with live demos, code, props and behavior notes, plus five working sector prototypes built on one token foundation.
