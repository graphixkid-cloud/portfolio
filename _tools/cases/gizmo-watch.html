Verizon Gizmo Watch

A smartwatch worn by a six-year-old and bought, set up and trusted by a parent.

My role
Lead / Principal Experience Designer across Gizmo Watch 2, 3 and 4, with my deepest involvement on Gizmo Watch 4: end-to-end UX/UI architecture, hardware-software interaction, on-watch OS navigation and the GizmoHub companion app. Also authored the UX analysis and product roadmap.
Scale
700K+ active parent accounts managing 2M+ connected family contacts

The problem

The watch is worn by a child but bought, configured and trusted by a parent. Every new feature had to be simple enough for a six-year-old's wrist and clear enough to earn a parent's confidence from the companion app.

Key decisions

  1. Design every feature as a pair. Each feature (Ask & Learn AI assistant, weather, alerts, video calling and texting, water lock, to-do lists) has an on-watch interaction and a GizmoHub counterpart for the parent, designed together.
  2. Keep the wrist simple and put control with the parent. The watch shows a child only what they need in the moment. Setup, rules and trusted contacts (up to 10–20 per watch, including multiple guardians) live in GizmoHub.
  3. Design for a child who grows. The feature set extends the watch's useful life as children age into it, instead of being outgrown in a year.
